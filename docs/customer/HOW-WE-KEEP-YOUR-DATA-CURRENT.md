# How often we check each event, and why

DRAFT for the operator to read and send. Not yet in effect: the new schedule starts after the operator confirms the date. Written for the customers of the weekly Conference Review (Arnica and Utility Global).

## The short version

We check every event you track on a schedule that matches how quickly its facts can change. Events where a deadline, a status or a venue can change this week are checked every week. Events where nothing can change for a while are checked less often. Every check we skip carries forward the last information we confirmed, unchanged, so a row on your page never goes blank or gets older in a way you cannot see.

## How the schedule works

| What the event is doing | How often we check it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A call for speakers is open, upcoming, or needs verification | Every week | Deadlines move, calls open and close, and links break. This is the information you act on, so it gets the most attention. |
| The call has closed and the event is within 30 days | Every week | In the last month before an event, venue, schedule, cancellation and sponsorship news arrive quickly. |
| The call has closed and the event is 31 to 90 days away | Every 2 weeks | Changes still happen, but rarely week to week. |
| The call has closed and the event is more than 90 days away | Every 4 weeks | With months to go, the facts change slowly. A date or venue change is the main thing to catch. |
| The event has just ended | One check 2 to 3 weeks after it ends, then none until day 60 | Organisers publish the final dates and results a few weeks after an event, so that is when a check is worth doing. Nothing useful changes in the weeks between. |
| The event ended more than 60 days ago | Every 2 weeks | Organisers announce next year's dates months after the event. Checking regularly means you hear about the next edition soon after it is announced. |

## What always triggers a fresh check, whatever the schedule says

- A link on your page stops working.
- You tell us an event needs a fresh look.
- Our team, or the people who supply our research, flag a specific event.
- A new year begins: in January every event is checked once.
- An event we have never checked before is checked right away.

## What does not change

- Every event on your sheets stays on your page and keeps its last confirmed information when a check is skipped. Nothing is blanked or removed to save effort.
- A deadline is marked verified only when we can point to the page that says so; otherwise it is labelled projected. A skipped check does not weaken that, and does not change a row's label.
- You can ask for a fresh look at any event at any time, and we will include it in the next weekly run.

## Why we think this is the right balance

A check that finds nothing new tells you nothing, and it does not make your information more accurate. Spending that effort where facts actually move means the things you act on (open calls, nearby events, links, new editions) are looked at more often and more carefully than if every event were checked equally. We will review this schedule against what the checks actually find, and tighten it for any kind of event where we see changes being missed.
